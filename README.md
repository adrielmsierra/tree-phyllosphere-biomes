## Decoupling of leaf economic strategies and phyllosphere microbial functional assembly across tropical and temperate biomes

This repository contains the processes phyllosphere metagenomic datasets. The bioinformatic workflow includes the Anvi'o metagenomic workflow (contigs assembly, profiling and annotations), R code and statistical analytical workflows for studying tree phyllosphere microbiome across biomes

Adriel M. Sierra & Geneviève Lajoie

## Abstract
1. Plant-microbe interactions are central to species adaptation and resource allocation. Plant functional traits are known to govern acquisitive to conservative resources strategies trade-offs, which have been correlated to phyllosphere microbial community. The extent to which these strategies determine both the composition and function of plant-associated microbial communities and whether they do so consistently across biomes remains unclear. 
2. Here, we investigate functional assembly of phyllosphere tree microbiomes in two contrasting forest biomes: lowland tropical forest in Panama and temperate deciduous forest in northeastern North America. By comparing shotgun metagenomes of phyllosphere microbiomes from seventeen tropical and twenty-four temperate tree species, we assess how forest biome and tree resource strategies shape the taxonomic and functional assembly of bacteria across phylogenetically diverse hosts and shape both the functional diversity and redundancy of leaf bacterial communities. 3. Tropical tree phyllospheres exhibited significantly higher functional alpha diversity and distinct functional composition compared to temperate tree phyllospheres. Tropical microbiomes were characterized by metabolic pathways related to glycolysis, pentose phosphate pathway, fatty acid degradation, and regulatory mechanisms, whereas temperate microbiomes were characterized by functions related to specialized energy conservation (e.g., oxidative phosphorylation, sulfur metabolism). However, functional variation across the ordination space and specific pathway functional redundancy percentage, revealed that phyllosphere functional traits were partially decoupled from categorical LES resource strategies. Instead, linear correlation and multi-trait phylogenetic models demonstrated that other host functional traits (primarily leaf phosphorus) explain microbial functional specialization in tropical forests, supporting an overlap of host-microbiome mutualistic resource use.
